# Deep Sleep Wakeup

**Developed by Amine Saoud ibn al-Bashir.**

Battery projects spend most of their life waiting. This example shows how the NanoKit Integrated ESP32 sleeps with its CPU and RAM switched off, wakes up on a timer or on a button press, and still remembers how many times it has booted.

## Goal

Put the ESP32 into deep sleep for 10 s, wake it earlier with a push button on an RTC-capable GPIO, blink the onboard LED at every wake-up, and print a boot counter that survives sleep together with the reason the chip woke up.

## What You Will Learn

- What deep sleep switches off (CPU, RAM, peripherals) and what stays alive (the RTC domain).
- Why a wake-up button must sit on an RTC GPIO such as GPIO33, and which NanoKit pins qualify.
- How `RTC_DATA_ATTR` keeps a variable alive across deep sleep when every other variable is lost.
- How to arm two wake-up sources at once with `esp_sleep_enable_timer_wakeup` and `esp_sleep_enable_ext0_wakeup`.
- Why `esp_sleep_get_wakeup_cause` is checked at the top of `setup()`, and why `loop()` is never reached.
- Why `Serial.flush()` must run before `esp_deep_sleep_start()`.

## How It Works

1. Every start of `setup()` increments `bootCount`, a variable marked `RTC_DATA_ATTR` so it lives in RTC slow memory, which stays powered during deep sleep.
2. `esp_sleep_get_wakeup_cause()` tells the program whether it started from the timer, from the button (ext0), or from a plain power-on or reset, and the answer is printed.
3. The onboard LED on NanoKit Pin 17 / GPIO2 blinks three times so a wake-up is visible even without the Serial Monitor.
4. `esp_sleep_enable_timer_wakeup(10 s)` arms the RTC timer and `esp_sleep_enable_ext0_wakeup(GPIO_NUM_33, 1)` arms the button: the chip wakes when GPIO33 goes HIGH.
5. `Serial.flush()` waits until every queued byte has really left the UART, then `esp_deep_sleep_start()` powers the chip down. The next wake-up is a fresh reset that runs `setup()` again, so `loop()` never executes.

## Expected Output

```text
NanoKit deep sleep wake-up example.
Boot number: 1
Wake-up cause: power-on / reset (not a deep-sleep wake-up)
Wake button on NanoKit Pin 20 / GPIO33 reads LOW (released, pull-down OK)
Blinking the onboard LED on NanoKit Pin 17 / GPIO2 three times.
Timer wake-up armed for 10 s. Button wake-up armed on NanoKit Pin 20 / GPIO33 (HIGH level).
Entering deep sleep now.
NanoKit deep sleep wake-up example.
Boot number: 2
Wake-up cause: timer (sleep period elapsed)
...
NanoKit deep sleep wake-up example.
Boot number: 3
Wake-up cause: external button on GPIO33 (ext0)
```

Between two blocks the Serial Monitor also shows a few lines that begin with `ets` and `rst:0x5 (DEEPSLEEP_RESET)`. They are printed by the chip's boot ROM at every wake-up, not by this program, and they confirm that the reset really came from deep sleep.

## Safety Notes

The button connects the GPIO to 3.3 V (NanoKit Pin 13 or 14), never to 5 V (NanoKit Pin 11 or 32): ESP32 GPIOs are 3.3 V only and 5 V on GPIO33 can destroy the input. Keep the 10 kOhm pull-down in place; without it the pin floats, the chip may wake at random, and the "reads HIGH" hint at boot will tell you so. Do not connect anything else to NanoKit Pin 20 / GPIO33 while this example runs.
