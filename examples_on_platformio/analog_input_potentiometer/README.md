# Analog Input Potentiometer

**Developed by Amine Saoud ibn al-Bashir.**

The first analog input for NanoKit Integrated ESP32: read the voltage on a potentiometer wiper with the ADC, turn it into a number, and show it three ways. Everything that measures a voltage later, from light sensors to battery monitors, starts here.

## Goal

Read a 10 kOhm potentiometer on an ADC1 pin, average 16 samples, print the raw count, the calibrated millivolts, a percentage, and a text bar every 100 ms, and dim the onboard LED to match the knob position.

## What You Will Learn

- How `analogRead` turns a voltage between 0 V and 3.3 V into a number from 0 to 4095.
- What `analogSetPinAttenuation(pin, ADC_11db)` selects and how attenuation trades range for precision.
- What `analogReadMilliVolts` adds on top of the raw count and where its calibration comes from.
- Why GPIO34 (ADC1) was chosen instead of an ADC2 pin, and what Wi-Fi has to do with it.
- What an input-only pin is and why it suits a potentiometer perfectly.
- Why averaging several samples gives a steadier reading, and where the ESP32 ADC is not linear.

## How It Works

1. The potentiometer is a voltage divider: one outer leg sits at 3.3 V, the other at GND, and the wiper picks any voltage in between as the knob turns.
2. `analogSetPinAttenuation(POT_PIN, ADC_11db)` tells the ADC to accept inputs up to about 3.3 V on this pin. The Arduino core already defaults to 11 dB, but writing it out makes the range explicit and shows where to switch to `ADC_0db` (about 0-1.1 V) when a small signal needs finer steps.
3. Every 100 ms, `loop()` reads GPIO34 sixteen times with `analogRead` and sixteen times with `analogReadMilliVolts`, then averages both.
4. The raw count (0-4095) is turned into a percentage and into an 8-bit duty by dropping its four lowest bits.
5. `ledcWrite` sets the onboard LED brightness from that duty, and one line with the raw count, millivolts, percent, and a 20-character bar is printed.

```bash

```

The millivolt column comes from the factory calibration, so it will not be exactly `raw * 3300 / 4095`; near both ends of the range the two drift apart, which is normal for the ESP32 ADC.

## Safety Notes

Power the potentiometer from 3V3 (NanoKit Pin 13 or Pin 14), never from 5 V (NanoKit Pin 11 or Pin 32): a wiper at 5 V would push 5 V into GPIO34, and NanoKit GPIOs are 3.3 V only. Check that the two outer legs go to 3V3 and GND and the middle leg to the GPIO. If the wiper is swapped with an outer leg, the reading sticks at one end and, at one extreme of the knob travel, the wiper nearly shorts 3V3 to GND through the track, which loads the board regulator.
