# Hall Sensor

**Developed by Amine Saoud ibn al-Bashir.**

The ESP32 chip carries a small Hall-effect sensor that reacts to magnetic fields. This example needs no wiring at all: bring a magnet near the metal shield of the NanoKit-iM module and watch the onboard LED and the Serial Monitor respond.

## Goal

Measure the "no magnet" level of the built-in Hall sensor at start-up, average the noisy readings, print the change in the reading together with a polarity word, and light the onboard LED whenever a magnet is close enough.

## What You Will Learn

- What a Hall-effect sensor measures and why the sign of the reading flips with the magnet pole.
- Why the raw `hallRead()` value is noisy and how averaging tames it.
- Why a start-up baseline is better than a fixed number copied from someone else's board.
- Which two NanoKit pins the sensor borrows (Pin 4 / GPIO39 and Pin 5 / GPIO36) and why they must stay free.
- That `hallRead()` belongs to Arduino-ESP32 core 2.x and disappeared in core 3.x.

## Hardware Required

| Item | Quantity | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| NanoKit Integrated ESP32 | 1 | Main controller board; the Hall sensor and the LED are both on board |
| Small permanent magnet | 1 | A fridge magnet or a neodymium disc; stronger is easier |

No jumper wires, no breadboard, no resistor.

`hallRead()` does not use a header pin of its own, so it has no row in the table. It reads the sensor through ADC1 channels 0 and 3, which are NanoKit Pin 5 / GPIO36 and NanoKit Pin 4 / GPIO39: leave both pins unconnected while this example runs.

## How It Works

1. `setup()` averages 64 `hallRead()` samples with no magnet nearby; the result is the baseline for this specific chip.
2. Every 100 ms, `loop()` averages 16 samples and computes `delta = value - baseline`.
3. If `|delta|` is larger than `DETECT_THRESHOLD` (30 counts), the onboard LED on NanoKit Pin 17 / GPIO2 turns on.
4. The sign of `delta` picks the polarity word: a positive delta is printed as "north face", a negative one as "south face". Which sign belongs to which pole depends on how the module sits on the board, so calibrate once with a magnet whose poles you know.
5. Each reading prints the value, the baseline, the delta, and the polarity word.

## Build and Upload

```bash
pio run
pio run --target upload
pio device monitor -b 115200
```

## Expected Output

```text
NanoKit Hall-effect sensor example.
No wiring needed. Keep NanoKit Pin 4 / GPIO39 and Pin 5 / GPIO36 unconnected: hallRead() uses them.
Measuring the baseline: keep magnets away from the module...
Baseline: 18
Bring a magnet near the metal shield of the NanoKit-iM module. LED on NanoKit Pin 17 / GPIO2 lights on detection.
value: 19 | baseline: 18 | delta: 1 | no magnet
value: 16 | baseline: 18 | delta: -2 | no magnet
value: 97 | baseline: 18 | delta: 79 | north face (positive delta)
value: 142 | baseline: 18 | delta: 124 | north face (positive delta)
value: -88 | baseline: 18 | delta: -106 | south face (negative delta)
value: 17 | baseline: 18 | delta: -1 | no magnet
```

The baseline and the size of the delta differ from chip to chip and from magnet to magnet; what matters is a delta that stays near zero without a magnet and swings clearly in one direction or the other when the magnet approaches.

## Safety Notes

There is no electrical risk in this example: nothing is connected to the header. Keep strong magnets away from magnetic storage media and from the USB cable's connector, and do not let a metal magnet touch the pins of the header, where it could short two of them together. Leave NanoKit Pin 4 and Pin 5 free: any signal connected there would corrupt the Hall reading.
