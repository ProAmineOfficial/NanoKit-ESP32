# Touch Sensor

**Developed by Amine Saoud ibn al-Bashir.**

The ESP32 inside the NanoKit Integrated ESP32 has ten capacitive touch inputs built in. This example turns a single jumper wire into a touch button: no switch, no resistor, just a wire and a finger.

## Goal

Measure the untouched reading of touch channel T0 at start-up, derive a threshold from it, light an LED while the pad is touched, print the live value, and show how the same channel can raise a hardware interrupt instead of being polled.

## What You Will Learn

- How capacitive touch sensing works and why the `touchRead()` value *drops* when a finger approaches.
- Why the baseline must be measured on each board and wire instead of being hard-coded.
- How to turn a baseline into a threshold with a ratio and a floor.
- How to poll the pad with `millis()` and drive an LED without `delay()`.
- How `touchAttachInterrupt()` lets the touch hardware wake the program by itself, and what an ISR may and may not do.
- Which NanoKit pins carry the ten touch channels T0 to T9.

## How It Works

1. During the first second after boot, `setup()` averages 32 `touchRead()` values while nobody touches the wire; this average is the baseline.
2. The threshold is 70 % of the baseline, and at least 10 counts below it (whichever is lower). A reading under the threshold means "touched".
3. Every 50 ms, `loop()` reads the pad, compares the value with the threshold, and switches GPIO26 HIGH (LED on) or LOW (LED off).
4. `touchAttachInterrupt()` hands the same threshold to the touch hardware, which raises an interrupt whenever a measurement falls below it. The ISR only sets a flag; `loop()` counts the flags.
5. Every 250 ms a status line prints the value, the baseline, the threshold, the state, and the interrupt counter.

## Expected Output

```text
NanoKit touch sensor example.
Touch pad: NanoKit Pin 10 / GPIO4 (T0). LED: NanoKit Pin 37 / GPIO26.
Measuring the baseline: do NOT touch the pad during the first second...
Baseline: 74 | threshold: 64
Touch the pad: the LED lights and the status line changes.
value: 74 | baseline: 74 | threshold: 64 | state: released | ISR events: 0
value: 73 | baseline: 74 | threshold: 64 | state: released | ISR events: 0
value: 31 | baseline: 74 | threshold: 64 | state: TOUCHED | ISR events: 6
value: 29 | baseline: 74 | threshold: 64 | state: TOUCHED | ISR events: 14
value: 74 | baseline: 74 | threshold: 64 | state: released | ISR events: 15
```

The absolute numbers differ from board to board and wire to wire; what matters is the clear drop while the finger is on the pad. The interrupt counter keeps climbing while the finger stays, because the touch hardware re-triggers at every measurement cycle in which the value is still below the threshold.

## Safety Notes

The touch wire carries nothing but a tiny measuring signal at 3.3 V level, so it is safe to touch. Never connect the touch wire to any voltage source, and never connect the LED directly between a GPIO and GND without its resistor. Keep the free end of the wire away from the 5 V rail on NanoKit Pin 11 or 32.
